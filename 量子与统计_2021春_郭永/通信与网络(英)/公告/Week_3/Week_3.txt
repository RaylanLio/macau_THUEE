内容: For the remaining part of information theory, we derive a simpler version of Shannon's formula, which is the upper bound of the bits of information one can transmit over an AWGN channel (per channel use). The presence of noise is the underlying reason why we cannot extract an infinite data rate from the entropy of a cont. r.v..
If you need a solid review, please refer to Haykin - Digital Communication Systems 5.1-5.10
 
In the real world, analog signals are continuous in time and value, equivalent to an infinite number of cont. r.v., thus it seems hopeless to transmit a general cont. signal via communications.
However, for bandwidth-limited(lowpass) signals, if we sample beyond the Nyquist sampling rate, we can perfectly recover the signal with the 'sinc' interpolation function. Much lower sampling rates are achievable for bandpass signals, yet it is important to note that there could be exceptions: sampling rates higher than the minimum rate may still lead to aliasing, referring to the diagram of feasible regions.
Supplementary readings: Nguyen - A First Course in Digital Communications 4.1.1, *Proakis - Digital Signal Processing 6.4.1 
 
Quantization, turning cont. r.v. to disc. r.v., always suffers from quantization noise. For uniform quantizer, an additional bit increases the quantization SNR by 6dB (4 times). Since the statistics of quantization error are the same across the whole quantization range, weaker signals pick up relatively more noise than stronger signals. Intuitively, in a better design, the quantization step sizes should be proportional to the amplitudes, which leads to the robust quantizer we will learn next week.
Supplementary readings: Nguyen - A First Course in Digital Communications 4.3.1
