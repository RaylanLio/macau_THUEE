内容: To transmit a bit sequence in a real-world communication system, we need to modulate it to an analog signal. 
First, bits are mapped to symbols. Real number symbols are for baseband modulation, e.g. ASK, PAM, while complex numbers are for passband modulation, e.g. QAM, PSK. When encoding multiple bits, Gray encoding is leveraged to minimize the number of bit errors. For more information, please refer to Proakis - Digital Communications 3.2.
Then, symbols are carried by waveforms. However, for the band-limited waveform, the time scale is infinitely long, which induces inter-symbol inference. Intuitively, to avoid interference, if we use the sinc function as the waveform, we can sample only at zero positions of all the other symbols. To generalize, the necessary and sufficient condition for no-ISI is the Nyquist criterion: shifted replicas of the G(f) is a constant.
One practical design of a waveform that satisfies the Nyquist criterion is raised cosine filter, used in 2G GSM. It is important to understand the relationship between the bandwidth and the symbol rate. For more information, please refer to Proakis - Digital Communications 9.2.1.
After all, the total data rate of a real communication system is (symbol rate) * (bits/symbol). The enemies are ISI and noise level, respectively.
